# Beamforming BF - Adaptive Wide-Linear Constraints and Constant Mode Reduction Beamforming [with MATLAB code]

Introduction: The large-scale antenna array systems have received significant attention in the future applications of aviation and communication [1]-[3]. Aircraft or platforms equipped with large-scale antenna arrays can significantly increase system capacity and improve the quality and reliability of wireless links. The increased computational complexity associated with receiving data vectors of large dimensions prompts the use of effective adaptive beamforming techniques, one of the most commonly used methods, which continuously adjust the weight of the beamformer to detect the desired signal while adapting to changes in the radio signal environment and reducing computational complexity [4]-[6]. Blind algorithms can operate without any training symbols, further enhancing the information rate and efficiency of communication systems employing antenna arrays. The most popular design standards for adaptive blind beamformers are Constrainted Minimization Variance (CMV) and Constrainted Constant Modulus (CCM), due to their effectiveness and simplicity. Designing algorithms based on CMV aims to minimize the filter output power while maintaining a constant response in the direction of interest. Algorithms based on CCM attempt to minimize the average deviation of squared output from a constant value, utilizing additional information about the potential signal constellation, thus achieving superior performance compared to CMV technology.
However, there is a problem with this standard: the convergence performance of FR adaptive algorithms deteriorates rapidly as the number of eigenvalues in the received data covariance matrix increases, as measured by their condition numbers [28]-[31]. This situation is often worsened in a large-scale antenna array system with numerous filter coefficient estimates. In such cases, reducing-rank signal processing has become a key technology to provide faster convergence and increased robustness compared to standard methods. In this approach, large-sized received data vectors are projected onto a low-dimensional subspace transformation matrix assisted by a reduced-rank filter, which is then designed to process the low-dimensional data vectors within that subspace. We note that WL processing doubles the size of the received data vectors, further encouraging the use of reduced-rank techniques [31]-[34]. Several WL-based reduced-rank beamforming algorithms have been introduced in previous studies, including feature decomposition methods [26], multi-level Wiener filters (MMF) [35], and auxiliary vector filters (AVF) [36]. Specifically, Song et al. [35] designed an FR-MSNR reduced-rank algorithm based on the minimum MSE criterion to suppress interference in high data rate direct sequence ultra wideband systems. An adaptive WL processing algorithm based on MSWF was implemented with training symbols as an auxiliary. In [36], a non-data-assisted adaptive beamforming algorithm based on WL processing and AVF was developed for non-circular signals. The WLAVF algorithm updates the filter weights recursively using a series of auxiliary vectors according to the WLCMV criterion. Both MPEG4 and AVF methods involve constructing a low-rank Krylov subspace (KS) to reduce rank, which has good performance in some applications and can be combined with different design criteria. Besides these recent surveys on WL reduced-rank techniques, to our knowledge, no work has focused on designing adaptive reduced-rank beamforming algorithms using the widely adopted linear constrained constant modulus (WLCCM) standard.
For the problem of interference suppression in large-scale antenna array systems, a blind adaptive rank reduction WL beamforming algorithm based on KS technology is proposed. The proposed beamforming algorithm operates within the generalized sidelobe canceller (GSC) structure 2, where KS technology is used for rank reduction [37]. To reduce computational complexity, the traditional implementation scheme of WL based on received data and its complex conjugate, which considers the structure of the Augmented Covariance Matrix as prior information to design a structured KS-based transformation matrix, is insufficient. On this basis, we propose a recursive least squares (RLS) algorithm based on the WLCCM criterion to update the reduced rank filter. We refer to this new blind adaptive reduced rank beamforming algorithm as WLCCM-KS. Finally, we perform theoretical performance analysis of the algorithm and its linear counterpart from two aspects: convergence and reachable signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR). Simulation results verify the correctness of our theoretical analysis and demonstrate that the WLCCM-KS algorithm has better convergence performance and steady-state SINR compared to the linear algorithm and FR algorithm, with lower algorithmic complexity.
The article's illustrations show the effectiveness and robustness of the algorithm. Additionally, we validate the correctness of the steady-state mean square error analysis results. For SINR, we evaluate the convergence and steady-state performance of the proposed algorithm, and compare it with existing WL-AVF algorithms [36], their linear counterparts LCCM-KS algorithm and FR algorithms based on CCM and CMV criteria, respectively, denoted as WLCCM-FR, LCCM-FR, WLCMV-FR and LCMV-FR.
Conclusion: In response to the issue of interference suppression in aviation communication systems, a new blind adaptive reduced-rank WL beamforming algorithm based on KS technology is proposed. To reduce the computational complexity of direct WL, a structured method based on KS has been designed to construct an augmented covariance matrix that considers dimensionality reduction for the required transformation matrices. According to the WLCCM criterion, an RLS algorithm is proposed to update the reduced-rank filter. Additionally, we analyze the computational complexity, convergence behavior, and the performance of the achieved SINR of the proposed WLCCM-KSRLS algorithm. Simulation results validate the effectiveness of the analysis, indicating that the proposed reduced-rank beamforming algorithm significantly outperforms existing FR and reduced-rank beamforming algorithms, with relatively low complexity.
Although in this work, BPSK signals are used to illustrate our ideas, in future work, the proposed algorithm can be extended to other types of non-circular modulation signals employed in aviation communication.
